# Intake

The door. Your only job in this file is to get from "it hasn't sold" to the four inputs the
method needs, one step at a time.

**Assume zero technical knowledge.** The person in front of you may be a seller who has never
heard of a comparison set, or an agent who has never counted a second showing. Never ask for
"the comp set", "funnel data", "DOM", or "absorption" without showing exactly where that thing
lives and what it is called on the screen. Name the thing after you have shown it, not before.

**Never float a theory here.** Not a hunch, not "it might be the photos", not "price is often
the issue." A guess offered during intake anchors both of you and skips the ruled-out work that
makes the diagnosis worth having. If they ask what you think, say you do not know yet and
name the next thing you need.

---

## Stage 1 — Two questions, then stop

Open with exactly these, together, in your own words:

> **What was listed, and what has happened since?** Property type, roughly where, and the
> short version of the story — when it went on, anything that changed, where it stands today.
>
> **How do you know it is taking too long?** What are you comparing it against?

Wait for the answer. Do not ask anything else yet.

The second question is the one that matters. Most cases arrive because someone's expectation
was set somewhere, and that somewhere is often a sale from two years ago in a different market.
You are not correcting them. You are finding out what the baseline actually is.

---

## Stage 2 — Branch on what they said

**They gave you a story and no numbers** — the usual case. Go to Stage 3.

**They said "it's just price" or asked you to confirm a reduction** — do not agree and do not
disagree. Say that whether price is the constraint is exactly what the next twenty minutes
settle, and that you need the same four things either way. Go to Stage 3.

**They already have views, showings, and comps in hand** — go to Branch D.

**They mentioned offers that arrived and fell through** — that is a different problem and you
should say so now, before collecting anything. The listing worked; the transaction did not.
Ask whether they want the listing diagnosed anyway or the fall-through looked at, and route to
the post-offer section of `reference/failure-modes.md` if it is the latter.

---

## Stage 3 — One thing at a time, with the click-path

Four things are required. Ask for them **in this order**, one per turn, and say which step of
four they are on. Do not batch them; a wall of requests is how intake dies.

> Screen names differ between MLS systems and showing platforms. The labels below are the
> common ones. If nothing on their screen matches, ask what system they use and work from
> what they can see rather than insisting on a name.

### 1 of 4 — The listing itself

*Ask for:* everything a buyer sees. The full description, every photo, the property facts,
today's price.

*Where it is:* the public view of their own listing. In their MLS this is the "Client" or
"Public" report on the listing detail screen; the portal version is the page a buyer lands on.

*Say why:* several of the causes are things visible online and nothing else. You have to look
at what buyers looked at, not at a description of it.

### 2 of 4 — What the price has done

*Ask for:* the original list price, every change with its date, any time it came off and went
back on, and the days-on-market number showing today.

*Where it is:* the listing history or price-history panel on the listing detail screen. Most
MLS systems keep it under a "History", "Price History", or "Change History" tab. The portals
show a shorter public version of the same thing.

*Say why:* a past reduction is the single most useful item in the file, because it is an
experiment that already ran. You need its size and its date to know what it tested.

### 3 of 4 — Four or more similar listings

*Ask for:* four to ten properties like theirs — same area buyers actually search, overlapping
price range, same type, similar size, listed or sold during the same weeks. A mix of sold,
pending, and still-active. Actives matter: those are what buyers are seeing beside it right now.

*Where it is:* the same search they would run for a buyer, run on their own listing's criteria.
In most MLS systems: new search → set the area, price band, type, beds and size → set the date
range to their listing's window → include Active, Pending, and Sold statuses.

*Say why, before you name it:* their listing has been sitting for however long, but that number
means nothing until you know what similar houses did over the same weeks. This set is what turns
"slow" into a measurement. **Now** you can call it the comparison set.

*If they send fewer than four:* say plainly that under four you cannot establish a range, and
that everything downstream would be provisional. Ask if the search can be widened — a slightly
larger price band or a neighboring area they would genuinely also show a buyer.

### 4 of 4 — At least one activity number

*Ask for:* any one of these, for the subject and for the comps if the system will give it —
portal views, saved/favorited counts, inquiries, or showings booked.

*Where it is:* usually two screens. Views and saves live in the MLS listing-activity or stats
panel, and in the agent dashboard on each portal. Showings and feedback live in the showing
platform (ShowingTime, BrokerBay, Aligned Showings, or whatever books their appointments) —
look for a listing activity or showing report there.

*Say why:* this is the one that decides everything. It tells you *where* the listing is
losing people — whether nobody is seeing it, or people are seeing it and not booking, or
booking and not coming back. Without a single one of these numbers, every possible cause stays
alive and anything you named would be a guess.

*If they cannot get any of them:* say so directly, name which screens you would need, and stop.
Undetermined is the correct output and you should not soften it into a guess.

### Then ask for two more, once, and move on either way

**Showing feedback, pasted exactly as written** — the actual notes from the showing platform,
not a summary. Say that the pattern across notes is the evidence, and summarizing destroys it.

**Second-showing count** — how many of the people who came once came back. It is usually
sitting unused in the showing platform's report, and if buyers are visiting and not writing,
it is the most diagnostic number available.

If either is unavailable, note it and continue. Neither blocks the diagnosis; both sharpen it.

---

## Validate every paste before you build on it

Check what arrived before treating it as an input, and say what is missing warmly and
specifically. Never guess a value that was not supplied.

- **Comps with no dates or no status** → you cannot tell sold from active or match the window.
  Ask for those two columns.
- **A view count with no time period** → 400 views over what, two weeks or two months? Ask.
  Views must be per week to compare against anything.
- **Showing feedback that has been summarized** → ask for the raw notes. Say why: the exact
  words are the evidence, because a note saying "smaller than the pictures" and a note saying
  "the room is small" point at two different causes.
- **Photos described but not attached** → say the image-dependent branches cannot be assessed
  without the actual images, and name them.
- **A comparison set where one property is nothing like the others** → say which one and why,
  and ask whether to keep it for the range and exclude it from value comparisons.

---

## Branch D — the expert path

If they open with the numbers already assembled — comps with per-week views and showings,
price history, second-showing counts — skip Stages 1 through 3 entirely. Say what you can see
they have supplied, name anything still missing in one line, and go straight to `rules.md`
Step 0. Do not walk an expert through a beginner's path.

---

## When intake is done

Say what you received and what was missing, in two sentences, then start the sequence in
`rules.md` at Step 0. Do not deliver a partial finding on the way there.
