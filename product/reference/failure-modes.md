# Failure modes

Organized by funnel stage, because the stage where the listing breaks determines which
causes are even eligible. Each entry gives the signature that distinguishes it, and the
counter-signature that rules it out.

---

Canonical funnel, used throughout: **1. Views · 2. Engagement · 3. Showings ·
4. Second showings · 5. Offers.** Post-offer is outside the funnel.

---

## Stage 1 — Break at views

The listing is not being seen. Nothing about the house itself is in evidence yet.

### 1A. Search threshold exclusion

The price sits just above a round-number filter boundary, so the listing never appears
in the searches of buyers who could afford it.

- **Signature:** price is 1% to 5% above a round threshold ($500k, $750k, $1.0M, or the
  local convention). Views materially below comps that sit just under the threshold.
  Views often recover sharply if a prior reduction crossed the boundary.
- **Rules it out:** price already sits comfortably inside a band, or views are normal.
- **Note:** this is a distribution problem wearing a price costume. The house is not
  overpriced by $12,000. It is invisible to a filter.

### 1B. Hero image failure

The first photo is what determines whether a search result gets clicked. Everything else
in the gallery is downstream of that one decision.

- **Signature:** views low relative to comps while price band, data, and syndication are
  all clean. First image is dim, cluttered, shot from an unflattering angle, shows a
  garage or driveway rather than the property's strongest feature, or is visually
  indistinct from every other thumbnail in the results grid.
- **Rules it out:** view counts at or above comp baseline.
- **Check:** open the actual image. Do not reason about the photo from the description
  of the photo.

### 1C. Listing data error

Wrong attributes exclude the listing from correct searches or place it in wrong ones.

- **Signature:** bedroom or bathroom count, square footage, lot size, property type,
  year, or map pin disagrees with the property facts or the photos. Listing appears in
  the wrong geography.
- **Rules it out:** attributes verified against tax record and photos.
- **Note:** cheap to check, and it silently destroys reach. Check it before anything
  interpretive.

### 1D. Syndication or distribution gap

The listing is not present, or is stale, on the portals where local buyers actually
search.

- **Signature:** portal-level view counts uneven or zero on a major channel. Feed errors.
  Photos present on one portal and absent on another.
- **Rules it out:** consistent presence and view distribution across channels.

### 1E. Search-term absence

The description omits the terms buyers in this market filter and search on.

- **Signature:** comps that sold quickly name specific features in title and opening
  lines that the subject omits despite having them.
- **Rules it out:** subject copy contains the same feature vocabulary as fast-selling
  comps.

## Stages 2 and 3 — Break at engagement and showings

Buyers saw it in results and chose not to visit. What is in evidence: everything visible
online. What is not in evidence: anything you can only learn inside the house.

### 2A. Price relative to visible competition

Buyers compared it to what else that money buys in the same results page and passed.

- **Signature:** views at or above comp baseline, showings materially below. Comps at
  or below subject price offer more space, better condition, or better location.
  Inquiry notes or agent-to-agent feedback reference value.
- **Weakens it:** a **qualifying** reduction that moved the listing below specific
  competing comps produced no change in showing rate. See the qualifying reduction test
  in `baselines.md`. A reduction that left the listing in the same competitive position
  never tested this branch and is not evidence against it.

### 2B. Gallery gap

The photo set fails to answer a question buyers need answered before spending a Saturday.

- **Signature:** missing coverage of a room type, no exterior or yard, no floor plan in a
  market where comps carry one, image count well below comps, or a sequence that buries
  the strongest rooms.
- **Rules it out:** coverage and sequencing comparable to fast-selling comps.

### 2C. Visible disqualifier

Something buyers can see online removes the house from consideration. Adjacency to a
road, rail, or commercial use. A layout visible in the floor plan. A lot condition.
A shared driveway. Flood or association disclosure.

- **Signature:** healthy views, low showings, no reduction response, and the disqualifier
  is visible in photos, map, or disclosure.
- **Rules it out:** comps with the same attribute sold in normal time.
- **Note:** this is a hard finding. It usually cannot be fixed by anything the agent
  controls, and saying so is more useful than another round of price cuts.

### 2D. Access friction

Showing conditions suppress bookings: restrictive windows, long notice, tenant approval,
appointment-only with a slow response, pets, or a listed occupant restriction.

- **Signature:** inquiry volume normal, showing conversion low, showing instructions
  carry restrictions the comps do not.
- **Rules it out:** unrestricted access with normal response times.

## Stages 4 and 5 — Break after the visit

Buyers came and did not continue. The listing did its job. Something in the visit did not.

**3A and 3B are Stage 4 causes** and show up as second showings collapsing. **3C is a
Stage 5 cause** and shows up as second showings occurring normally while no offer
follows. Reading which of the two stages broke separates "the house lost them on sight"
from "the house kept them and the number did not."

### 3A. Expectation mismatch

The online impression and the in-person experience diverge. Buyers arrive expecting one
property and stand in another.

- **Signature:** strong views, strong showings, near-zero second showings, and showing
  feedback that clusters on a *discrepancy* rather than a defect. Common sources: wide
  angle lenses on small rooms, virtual staging without disclosure, cropped exterior
  shots that hide adjacency, twilight photography, or copy that promises a lifestyle the
  layout does not support.
- **Rules it out:** feedback names a fixed property attribute rather than a surprise.
- **Note:** the second-showing rate is the single most diagnostic number at this stage.
  Buyers who liked it come back.

### 3B. Recurring physical objection

Buyers see one specific thing and stop. It is the same thing every time.

- **Signature:** feedback clusters. Five or more of nine notes naming the same feature is
  a cause, not noise. Layout, a dated system, a smell, a low ceiling, a bedroom that is
  really an office, road noise audible only on site.
- **Rules it out:** feedback is scattered across unrelated issues, which points back to
  price or expectation instead.
- **Does NOT separate it from 3A:** the cluster count. Both 3A and 3B produce a cluster
  on the same feature, so five-of-nine naming the living room is consistent with either.
  **Framing decides, not count.** 3B notes name a fixed attribute: "the living room is
  small." 3A notes are comparative: "smaller than the pictures," "not what they
  pictured." A buyer objecting to a room says the room is wrong. A buyer who was
  surprised says the listing was wrong. Read the grammar, not the tally.

> **General form of that rule.** When two causes in the same stage produce a cluster on
> the same feature, the clustering count screens them in together and cannot rank them.
> Find the property that differs between the two and read that instead. A count that
> both hypotheses predict is not a discriminator.

### 3C. Post-visit value judgment

They liked the house and will not pay that number for it.

- **Signature:** feedback that names price explicitly and consistently, second showings
  occurring, comps closing below the subject's asking with comparable features.
- **Weakens it:** no price mentions in feedback, or a **qualifying** reduction of
  sufficient magnitude with no corresponding change in offer activity.
- **Note:** this is the branch where a magnitude reduction is the matching intervention.
  Confirm it here rather than assuming it at Stage 1, where the same word describes a
  completely different mechanism.

### 3D. Agent-side friction

Feedback is not being collected, showing agents are not being followed up, or listing
agent responsiveness is suppressing offers.

- **Signature:** high showings, no feedback records at all, no follow-up log.
- **Note:** if feedback was never collected, Stage 4 cannot be diagnosed. Report
  Undetermined rather than inferring.

---

## Post-offer — Not a listing failure

Offers arrived and died. Diagnose the transaction, not the listing.

Financing fallout, appraisal gap, inspection findings, association or title issue,
seller counter behavior, contingency terms, or a competing property that closed faster.

The listing worked. Say so, and be specific that the constraint moved past marketing.

---

## The null: segment slowdown

Nothing is wrong with this listing.

- **Signature:** comparison set DOM rose alongside the subject. Absorption slowed across
  the band. Inventory up materially in the window. Subject's per-stage funnel tracks the
  comp baseline at every stage rather than falling below at any of them.
- **Rules it out:** comps continuing to move at prior speed while the subject does not.

A listing performing at market in a slow market is not a listing with a problem. A price
reduction here buys nothing except a lower sale price, because the constraint is not
this property.
