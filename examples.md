# Examples

Three worked cases. They show the reasoning, not just the conclusion.

The cases are constructed for teaching, with figures chosen to make each discriminator
legible. They are not client files. The third one produces no defect, which is the
outcome most audit tools cannot reach and the one that saves the most money.

Between them they also show the three possible verdicts on a prior price reduction: one
qualifying reduction that returned a negative result, one case with no reduction to read,
and one non-qualifying reduction that is treated as no evidence at all.

---

## Case 1 — Break at Stage 4

**Input:** 4/2 single family, 58 days on market, one reduction at day 29. Comparison set
of eight in the same school zone, overlapping band, same twelve weeks. Portal views,
showing log, nine verbatim feedback notes.

### Failure observed

Listing active 58 days. Comparison set DOM ranges 11 to 41 days, six of eight closed.
Subject is beyond the top of the range. Failure confirmed.

### Comparison set

Eight properties, same school zone, $40k overlapping band, all listed within the
subject's window. Five sold, one pending, two active.

### Comparison-set integrity

**Usable.** Geography, band, type, size, and window all match. Condition tier comparable
across seven of eight, with one comp recently renovated. Removing that comp does not
change the DOM range materially or alter the second-showing baseline. No single comp is
carrying the baseline.

### Funnel reconstruction

| Stage | Subject (per week) | Comp range (per week) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Views | 312 | 240 to 380 | At baseline |
| 2. Engagement | 19 saves, 6 inquiries | 12 to 26 saves | At baseline |
| 3. Showings | 4.1 | 2.8 to 5.0 | At baseline |
| 4. Second showings | 1 of 34 first showings | 6 to 11 per 30 first showings | **Clearly below** |
| 5. Offers | 0 | 1 to 3 | Below, starved |

### Primary constraint

**Stage 4, second showings.** The listing performed at or above baseline through the
showing appointment. Buyers found it, clicked it, and came to the house at a normal rate.
One of 34 first showings produced a second visit, against a comp baseline of roughly one
in four.

Stages 1 through 3 are not the diagnosis site, and no conclusion is drawn about search
placement, price band positioning, or the gallery's ability to attract clicks. Those
mechanisms demonstrably worked. Stage 5 is starved and carries no independent information.

### Primary cause

**Expectation mismatch.** Seven of nine feedback notes name a discrepancy rather than a
defect, and six of those name the same one.

### Mechanism

The gallery's three main-room images are shot from a corner at wide angle with furniture
pushed to the walls, which reads as roughly a third larger than the stated square footage
supports. Buyers arrive holding a pre-visit expectation of scale that the walkthrough
contradicts in the first sixty seconds, and they resolve against the house on that visit
rather than leaving undecided. A buyer who leaves undecided comes back. These buyers are
not undecided.

### Evidence for this cause and against the alternatives

The second-showing rate is the discriminating figure. Buyers who like a house and intend
to negotiate return to it. A rate near zero against a comp baseline of one in four means
buyers are not leaving unresolved, they are leaving resolved.

The feedback clusters on surprise rather than on an attribute. "Smaller than it looked"
and "photos are misleading about the great room" describe a gap between two impressions.
A buyer objecting to a small living room says the room is small. These buyers are saying
they expected something else, which locates the fault in the representation rather than
in the house.

### Alternatives, and why they are demoted

**Price, comparison mechanism (Stage 3).** Not supported. Showings are at baseline, so
buyers are not filtering it out on price before visiting.

**Price, post-visit value mechanism (Stage 5).** Weakened, and the reduction is readable
here. The day-29 cut was $14,000 on a $479,000 list, which moved the subject below three
active comps it had previously sat above. The listing was otherwise unchanged, twenty-nine
days of observation follow it, and the comp set's activity was stable across the window.
That is a qualifying reduction, and it tested the comparative and post-visit mechanisms.
Second-showing rate did not move. **Strong evidence against price as the constraint at
the mechanisms this reduction was capable of testing.** It says nothing about search
thresholds, which are not in play here because Stage 1 is healthy.

**Gallery quality.** Not supported as a cause. The photos are performing at the stage
photos are supposed to perform at. The issue is what they represent, not how good they
are.

**Condition.** Not supported. No feedback names condition, systems, or maintenance.

### Null model

Rejected. Comparison set DOM did not rise across the window: three comps listed in the
back half sold in 14, 19, and 22 days. The subject's Stage 1 to 3 figures track the comp
baseline, which is itself evidence against a segment-wide slowdown, since a frozen market
suppresses views and showings first.

### Confidence

**Supported.** Comparison set is Usable, the constraint is located by two independent
measures, the feedback cluster separates the live branches, and the null was tested and
rejected.

### Missing evidence

Open house attendance was not supplied. No floor plan, and its absence may be
contributing at Stage 4 since four comps carry one.

### What would prove this wrong

Feedback showing buyers had seen the floor plan and dimensions before visiting would
weaken the mismatch reading substantially. So would a second-showing rate that is low
across the entire comparison set, which would make the subject's figure normal rather
than anomalous. That comp baseline was computed from six of eight comps and should be
verified against the full set.

## Case 2 — Break at Stage 1

**Input:** 3/2 townhouse, 47 days, no reductions. Comparison set of six. Portal views
broken out by channel.

### Failure observed

47 days against a comp range of 9 to 28. Beyond the range. Confirmed.

### Comparison set

Six properties, same district, same property type, all listed inside the subject's
47-day window. Price range $379,000 to $421,000 against the subject's $412,000. Size
1,340 to 1,620 sq ft against the subject's 1,410. Four sold, one pending, one active.
Two were listed in the final three weeks, which gives a late-window read.

### Comparison-set integrity

**Usable with limitations.** Five of six comps match on geography, band, type, and
window. The sixth is in an adjacent association with materially lower monthly dues,
which affects buyer affordability calculations at this band. It is retained for the DOM
range and excluded from any comparative-value reasoning. Confidence caps at Provisional
on branches that depend on comparative value. The Stage 1 finding does not depend on it.

### Funnel reconstruction

| Stage | Subject (per week) | Comp range (per week) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Views | 61 | 190 to 340 | **Clearly below** |
| 2. Engagement | 3 saves | 9 to 21 saves | Below, starved |
| 3. Showings | 0.6 | 2.1 to 4.4 | Below, starved |
| 4. Second showings | 0 of 4 | insufficient volume | Not assessable |
| 5. Offers | 0 | 0 to 2 | Starved |

### Primary constraint

**Stage 1, views.** Under a third of the lowest comp. Every subsequent stage is starved.

Nothing is concluded about the photographs, the condition, or the walkthrough. No
meaningful number of buyers reached any of them.

### Primary cause

**Search threshold exclusion.**

### Mechanism

The listing is priced at $412,000. Five of six comps sit between $379,000 and $398,000.
**The agent supplied the local filter convention with the case:** buyer search ceilings
in this band sit at $400,000 and $425,000. At $412,000 the listing is absent from every
search capped at $400,000, where the volume in this submarket sits, while appearing in
$425,000 searches against larger units it cannot win against. It is not competing badly.
In the searches that matter, it is not present.

*Note on why that attribution is written out.* A filter convention is a fact about a
local market, not something derivable from the case file. Asserting one you were not
given is inference dressed as evidence, which `reference/intake.md` prohibits. If the
convention is not supplied, threshold exclusion cannot be confirmed and stays tied with
the other live Stage 1 causes. Name the missing input instead of inventing the number.

### Evidence for this cause and against the alternatives

The view deficit is too large for a presentation cause. A weak hero image depresses
click-through on impressions that still occur. It does not remove a listing from the
results grid. A drop to under a third of the lowest comp indicates the listing is not
being served rather than being served and skipped.

Views are proportionally low across all three portals, which separates this from a
syndication gap. A feed problem produces uneven distribution.

Property attributes were verified against the tax record and the photos and match, which
rules out data-error exclusion.

### Alternatives, and why they are demoted

**Hero image.** Not supported at this magnitude. Untested, and remains live as a
secondary factor once visibility is resolved.

**Syndication.** Ruled out by even distribution across channels.

**Listing data error.** Ruled out by attribute verification.

**Description and search terms.** Not supported. The copy names the features the fast
comps name.

**Prior reduction.** None. There is no completed experiment to read here, which is worth
stating rather than leaving as an absence.

### Null model

Rejected. Comps moved at 9 to 28 days throughout the window, including two listed in the
final three weeks. The segment is not slow. The subject is uniquely unseen within it.

### Confidence

**Provisional.** The constraint is located cleanly and the mechanism is well supported,
but the comparison set carries a named limitation and impression data is unavailable.

### Missing evidence

Impression counts as distinct from view counts were unavailable on two portals. Those
would confirm the mechanism directly rather than by inference from the magnitude of the
deficit.

### What would prove this wrong

Impression data showing the listing is being served at comp-normal rates and simply not
clicked. That moves the diagnosis to the hero image and inverts the conclusion.

## Case 3 — Null

**Input:** 4/3 single family, 71 days, one reduction at day 41. Agent requesting support
for a second reduction. Comparison set of nine. Full funnel data.

### Failure observed

71 days on market. The seller expected 30, based on a neighborhood sale fourteen months
ago.

### Comparison set

Nine properties, same submarket and band, listed within the window. Two sold, three
pending, four active. DOM range 44 to 88, median 63.

### Comparison-set integrity

**Usable.** All nine match on geography, band, type, size, and window. Condition tier
comparable. No outlier dominates: removing the fastest and slowest comps leaves a range
of 51 to 79 and does not change the finding.

### Funnel reconstruction

| Stage | Subject (per week) | Comp range (per week) | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Views | 204 | 155 to 260 | At baseline |
| 2. Engagement | 11 saves, 4 inquiries | 7 to 16 saves | At baseline |
| 3. Showings | 2.4 | 1.6 to 3.1 | At baseline |
| 4. Second showings | 7 of 24 first showings | 5 to 9 per 24 | At baseline |
| 5. Offers | 0 | 0 to 1 | At baseline |

### Primary constraint

**None located.** The subject sits inside the comparison range at every stage of the
funnel.

### Primary cause

**No listing failure is demonstrated.**

### Mechanism

The segment slowed during the listing window. Comps from the first four weeks carry DOM
of 44 to 58. Comps listed after week six carry 67 to 88. The subject's 71 days sits inside
a range of 44 to 88, eight days above the median, and six of nine comps remain active or
pending after comparable time. The property is performing normally inside a market that
changed after the seller formed their expectation.

### Evidence for this cause and against the alternatives

A listing with a real constraint fails at an identifiable stage. This one does not. It
attracts views at baseline, converts them to showings at baseline, and produces second
showings at baseline, which means buyers are finding it, visiting it, and returning to it
at the rate this market is currently producing.

The seller's 30-day expectation comes from a transaction fourteen months old, outside the
comparison window, in a different rate environment. It is not a baseline.

### Alternatives, and why they are demoted

**Price.** Not supported, and the prior reduction is **uninformative** rather than
evidence either way. The day-41 cut was $7,500 on a $689,000 list. It did not cross a
search threshold and did not move the subject past any active comp, so it failed the
material-position-change condition and was never capable of testing a price mechanism.
The small view bump that followed is consistent with the refresh effect any status change
produces. Reading that bump as a partial price response, or reading the absent showing
response as proof the cut was too small, would both be over-readings of an experiment
that did not run.

**Presentation and condition.** Not supported. Both would appear as a below-range stage,
and no stage is below range.

### Null model

**Accepted. This is the diagnosis.** The comparison set's DOM rose across the window, the
subject tracks the set at every stage, and six of nine comps remain unsold at comparable
time.

### Confidence

**Provisional.** Nine comps, Usable integrity, and full funnel data across all five
stages, which is as clean as the comparison side of a case gets.

It caps here anyway, and the reason is worth stating rather than treating as a
technicality. The diagnosis *is* the null, and the null was inferred from the comparison
set's own DOM drift rather than tested against the input built to test it. Band-level
absorption and inventory were not supplied. So the leading alternative, that this comp
set is unrepresentative and the subject's apparent normality is an artifact of a badly
chosen comparison group, was never examined.

A null diagnosis that has not been checked against absorption data is exactly the case
where the cap earns its keep. Nothing about the funnel comparison is weak. The single
piece of evidence that would confirm the finding is missing, and confidence follows the
weakest load-bearing input rather than the average of the strong ones.

### Missing evidence

Band-level absorption and inventory figures were not supplied. They would confirm the
segment reading directly rather than by inference from the comp set's own DOM drift.

### What would prove this wrong

Absorption data showing the band is clearing at prior speed, which would mean the comp
set is unrepresentative and the subject's normality is an artifact of a badly chosen
comparison group.

---

**Why this case is in the set.** 
The requested output was support for a second reduction. The evidence does not support
one. No constraint specific to this property was found, so a reduction does not address
anything. What it does is lower the price the property eventually sells at, in a market
where comparable homes are also waiting.

Naming that is the diagnosis. What to do about a slow market is the agent's conversation
with their seller, and this folder does not have an opinion about it.
